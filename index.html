Teacher's Pet Methodology: Evaluates handoffs specifically to Will and Luke across 303 standups. 1. Opportunity Normalization: A member only has an opportunity to pick a teacher on days when both are present, the picker speaks before the teacher has already spoken, and the picker is not the final speaker. 2. Expected Baseline: Expected picks Exp = Σ [1 / (Remaining Candidates)]. Difference from Expected (Δ) isolates true favoritism from random baseline probability.

Sequence Mining & Grouping Methodology: We scan all 302 standup sessions in chronological order of nomination (P₁ → P₂ → … → PN). A contiguous sequence of length L ≥ 3 is recorded whenever it appears 2 or more times. Speaking Groups (Clusters): Unites all permutations of a contiguous cohort (e.g. A → B → C, B → C → A, C → A → B, and reverse sequences) to identify essential clusters of peers who reliably speak adjacent to each other. Reversible Corridors: Pairs reciprocal pathways (A ⇄ B ⇄ C) that flow back and forth across sessions. Maximal Scope: Filters out sub-slices subsumed entirely within larger repeated runs.

Every time a speaker reached for a teammate who had already spoken, we logged a mistake. Raw counts are misleading: the later your turn, the more people have already gone, so the easier it is to slip. Each teammate is therefore benchmarked against an exposure-adjusted expectation.

Mistakes Methodology: A slip-up is any turn flagged in the Mistakes column — the speaker tried to hand off to someone who had already spoken. Exposure: for a turn at position p in a session of N speakers, exposure = (p − 1) / (N − 1), the share of teammates already gone. Expected: a person's total exposure × the team-wide slips per unit of exposure. Δ = actual − expected, so a positive Δ means more slips than the position of their turns alone would explain.
